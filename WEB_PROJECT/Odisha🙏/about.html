About Odisha

Ancient Name:Kalinga:

Odisha is historically knoen as Kalinga, along with names such as Utkal and Odra.

Kalinga War(261 BCE):

The famous kalinga War was fought between Emporer Ashokaand the Mauryan Empire and Kalinga.

Ashoka's Transformation:

The massive suffering caused by the Kalinga War deeply affected Ashoka. He later embraced Buddhism and promoted peace and non-violence.

Dhauli:

Dhauli, near Bhubaneswar, is traditionally associated with the Kalinga War. Ashoka's rock edicts found there are important historical evidence.

Kharavela:

King Kharavela was one of ancient Kalinga's notable rulers. His achievements are recorded in the Hathigumphainscription at Udayagiri.

Udayagiri and Khandagiri Caves:

These caves near Bhubaneswar contain ancient rock-cut architecture and inscriptions associated with Jain traditions and Kharavela's period.

Maritime Trade:

Ancient Kalinga had strong maritime connectionswith regions of Southeast Asia. Odisha's sailors and merchants travelled across the Bay of Bengal.

Boita Bandana:

The festival of Boita Bandana commemorates Odisha's ancient maritime traditions. People traditionally float small boats during Kartika Purnima.

Eastern Ganga Dynasty:

The Eastern Ganga dynasty ruled large parts of Odisha and contributed greatly to its temple architecture and culture.

Jagannath Temple:

The present Shree Jagannath Temple at Puri was built mainly during the reign of Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva,beginning in the 12th century.

Konark Sun Temple:

The magnificent Konark Sun Temple was built in the 13th century during the reign of Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty.

Gajapati Kings:

During the 15th century, the Gajapati rulers, especially Kapilendra Deva, established a powerful kingdom based in Odisha.

Paika Rebellion (1817):

The Paika Rebellion was a major uprising against British rule in Odisha. Bakshi Jagabandhu was one of its prominent leaders.

Formation of Odisha Province (1936):

On 1 April 1936, Odisha became a separate province based largely on the Odia-speaking population. This is celebrated every year as Utkala Dibasa.

Odisha After Independence:

After India became independent in 1947, Odisha continued to develop as an Indian state, with Bhubaneswar becoming its capital in the modern period.
